Price Edge is edge ÷ fair — the frozen first-sighting edge as a share of what the contract costs, i.e. the model's expected ROI on stake if bought at the open. It is context, not a gate: the rule reads the point edge. Set Current rules and the Bet column re-decides every row against today's per-level edge floors, read live from the same config the Kalshi autotrader trades from — so this view and the bot cannot disagree about what is a pick today. Nothing frozen is rewritten: the verdict each row was scored with stays the graded book, and where the two views differ the Bet cell's tooltip says so and names the floor for that row's level. The differences run both ways here, because the slate shows every side: a level whose floor came down surfaces sides the old flat floor said NO to, and a level whose floor went up drops sides it said YES to. Floor (as scored) stays the default and remains the graded record. The split rule that used to occupy this switch is parked for 2027, paper only — its numbers live in FINAL_RULESET §1, not here.

Open to close is the market's opinion changing, not the model's. Fair already is the open — the feed carries Pinnacle's opening price and nothing re-quotes it, so every run of a day stores the same fair for a side and there is no intraday move to show. close is the closing line and Line move is close − settled open in points (since 4 Oct 2026, the backtest's definitions on both ends): on a feed-sourced row the settled open is the fair itself, and on a Pinnacle-sourced row it is the feed's reading of the open — usually the same number as our first tick (median gap 0.000 pts, measured 4 Oct), but where the two disagree it is the backtest's column that counts. A row with no settled open at all stays blank rather than grading on the feeler; fair is still on the row, so nothing raw is lost. Positive means the market came towards this side after it opened and the open was the better price. That is closing line movement, and it is deliberately not called CLV: closing line value is the close minus what was actually paid, a different and flattering number, because §2 rests a cent under fair and that discount alone is worth about a point. CLV in that sense lives on the Kalshi page, split from the discount. Both are blank until the match is graded and the close arrives, usually a day later, and blank for a void: a price a market was suspended at is not its last word. Line move is the honest scoreboard while the settled sample is still too small to read — it says whether you were on the right side of a moving line, on every bet rather than on the handful that have finished. A close marked * came from our own Pinnacle poller rather than the feed, because the feed never published that match — on 2026-09-14 that was 107 of 221. It is the last price Pinnacle showed before the match left the pre-match board, which is the same thing the feed's close is; the mark is there so the two are never silently mixed. † is the same source but a weaker claim: the poller lost sight of that match more than ten minutes before the advertised start, so the number is the last price it caught, not the last price on the board. Its line move is greyed, because the gap between those two is not a real move.

ROI is model ÷ price − 1, the expected return per unit at that price. quote is Pinnacle's raw vigged price, fair is the devigged open, and −1c/−2c are one and two points inside fair — the prices you would actually type into Novig. Placing an order moves the row onto the Book, which is where the venue, stake and fill get recorded.

Kalshi take is the same ROI if you cross the spread at fair on Kalshi instead of resting on Novig: model ÷ (fair + fee) − 1, with the taker fee 0.07 × p × (1−p) added to what you pay. It is the answer to "is it still worth it if I just take it now". Hover a cell for the fee in cents. The fee is a curve, not a haircut — it peaks at 1.75c at a coin-flip price and falls to about 1.1c at 0.20 or 0.80, so it eats a far larger share of a thin edge in the middle of the board than at the edges. Against the 2.5-point floor that is roughly 70% of the edge gone at 0.50 and 45% at 0.20. The maker fee is a quarter of it, 0.0175 × p × (1−p), which is why the frozen rule posts rather than takes.
